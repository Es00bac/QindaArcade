# The Reclaimed World: racers and machines

These are eight different machines built to the same racing footprint. Tire contact
points, collision bounds and driving rules remain equal. Ducké, Mochi and Axi are
smaller **drivers**, with raised seats and controls brought within reach; their
karts are not miniatures. All organizations and sponsors below are fictional.

## 01 / CyberPengu — Breakwater

**Role:** reluctant hero; municipal rescue technician, not a chosen celebrity.
When the old flood district lost power, Pengu kept the evacuation radios alive
with batteries pulled from the Bliss dump. The city replaced the district with a
billboard, but never replaced its rescue equipment. Pengu races to buy it back.
The **Open Current Cooperative** pays entry fees in return for public repair
manuals. Pengu quietly fixes rivals' brakes before the start.

**Machine:** a retired rescue tug cut down in a community workshop. Broad folded
teal armor, ivory rescue stripe, square lamp guards, a front recovery loop, rear
battery drawers, cargo grab rails and a little amber beacon. Salt-gray chips sit
on exposed panel edges; orange replacement fasteners mark the last rebuild.
The bumper reads **LEAVE NO ONE BEHIND**. Pengu built the drivetrain, but the
rescue crew signed the bodywork: heroism here is a team effort.

## 02 / Ducké — Pressure Drop

**Role:** brilliant, insufferable independent inventor; lovable rival, not villain.
The smallest apprentice at a shuttered laundry repaired its pressure governor
while everyone else argued about buying a new one. Ducké now insists that a
championship will prove the same thing about everything else. **Boilermate Works**
sponsors the kart with surplus valves; the contract explicitly forbids operating
it in the workshop kitchen. Ducké regards this as an endorsement.

**Machine:** a riveted mustard-yellow bathtub-shaped tub around an exposed copper
accumulator. Brass pressure gauges, twin short stacks, a red shutoff wheel,
scalloped mudguards and mismatched salvaged hubcaps. The nose was sprayed over
without removing the previous stencil. The rear says **MIND THE PRESSURE**.
Ducké welded it personally, and will explain every weld at unreasonable length.

## 03 / Vix — Redline Riot

**Role:** cocky street-racing heel with a conscience; talented and genuinely annoying.
Vix delivered medicine through the viaduct's night closures before discovering
that spectators paid more than dispatch. A viral victory brought **Afterhours
Audio** money and an ego almost as large as the rear wing. Vix wants a legal title
so the city cannot dismiss the district's drivers as vandals. Losing to a scrap
kart would be unbearable, especially if it belonged to a friend.

**Machine:** a chopped rally coupe nose, offset intake, flared angular sidepods,
wide splitter, rear intercooler, a tall wing and exposed tuned exhaust. Red over
charcoal, cream racing slashes, a spray-painted X and stone rash low on the nose.
One repaired fender is deliberately black. **LOUDER THAN YOUR EXCUSES** goes on
the bumper. Vix tunes it; the neighborhood body shop does the hard welding.

## 04 / Cache — Lost & Found

**Role:** opportunistic courier and information broker; a useful, unreliable ally.
Cache grew up cataloguing what the reclamation trucks called worthless. Every
parcel has an owner, every discarded circuit has another use, and every favor
has an interest rate. **Second Byte Salvage** funds the racing operation as a
rolling advert. Cache wants the prize to buy the yard before Hex's parent company
acquires it. Cache says it is a business decision; nobody quite believes that.

**Machine:** an asymmetric delivery flatbed. A blue reclaimed road-sign hood,
one cream replacement panel, a parcel cage, strapped tool chest, roofless cab
rails, antenna and a side-mounted spare. Repaired seams use visible fishplates.
Shipping labels and **NOT LOST. RELOCATED.** crowd the bumper. Cache assembled
the chassis from three donor carts and still knows where every bolt came from.

## 05 / Mochi — Moonshot

**Role:** quiet underdog and exacting engineer; kind, not timid.
Mochi maintained cable-car controls on Aurora Pass and practiced trajectories
on discarded survey paper. A rejected racing scholarship became a home-built
hillclimber. **Switchback Instruments**, a two-person calibration shop, supplies
bearing stock and a place to work. Mochi wants the mountain apprentices to see
someone like them on the podium, then return with enough money to reopen class.

**Machine:** a narrow lavender teardrop nose between open triangulated outriggers,
white aero endplates, exposed pushrods and a compact finned electric drive. Small
measuring ticks and precise stripes replace decorative clutter. One hand-sanded
primer patch survives on the tail. The sticker says **MEASURE TWICE. SEND ONCE.**
Mochi fabricated every bracket; the full-size chassis has a booster seat and
shortened steering column rather than a shrunken wheelbase.

## 06 / Hex — Hostile Takeover

**Role:** charming corporate villain; wins by calling exploitation efficiency.
Hex is the public face of **Vanta Dynamics**, the company buying the salvage
district parcel by parcel. The championship is a product demonstration: victory
would make the takeover look inevitable. Hex loves racing enough to resent the
engineers who actually designed the machine, and loves winning enough to keep
their names off it. The others are opponents; Pengu is a public-relations problem.

**Machine:** an angular satin-black prototype with magenta signature blades,
closed battery fairings, geometric wheel covers, a forked front splitter and
stacked rear diffusers. Almost immaculate, but the left rear carries a very real
crash repair that the team calls a development feature. **TERMS APPLY** is the
only joke on it. Expensive, severe, factory-built: its small wear marks must not
look like Cache's decades of accumulated repairs.

## 07 / Patches — Common Ground

**Role:** generous community mechanic with a stubborn competitive streak.
Patches repairs irrigation pumps in Reactor Gardens and never quite manages to
charge neighbors. The **Mend & Grow Union** turned a collection tin into a race
entry. Patches drives to secure the garden's water lease, and to show that repair
is a skill, not a consolation prize. Every rival has borrowed a wrench; all eight
will be reminded of this at the starting line.

**Machine:** a field-service buggy: olive enamel, cream and orange donor panels,
varnished timber cargo slats, welded steel bumpers, exposed vertical spring
mounts, an old radiator, tool roll and spare water can. Visible stitches of repair
plates, scraped lower rails and flower/union stickers accumulate without hiding
the underlying construction. **FIX IT. DON'T BIN IT.** is both bumper sticker
and operating philosophy. The neighborhood helped build it on Sunday afternoons.

## 08 / Axi — Little Current

**Role:** cheerful wetland scientist and environmental troublemaker; the heart of the field.
Axi mapped the streams under Bliss and found which abandoned electronics were
poisoning them. **Rill Research Collective** sponsors a racer because nobody
broadcasts water-quality meetings. Axi wants a mobile cleanup lab and an audience
large enough that the city cannot ignore the data. The smile is genuine. So is
the determination to beat Vanta on live television.

**Machine:** a rounded coral-and-seafoam research skiff converted to wheels,
twin low-side coolant pods, ribbed hoses, a strapped blue sample reservoir,
circular observation ports and a stern instrument mast. Small waterline scuffs,
salvaged cream patches, wave graphics and **SMALL BUT NOT OPTIONAL** stickers.
Axi designed the cooling circuit; Patches helped weld the frame. The little
driver sits on a raised waterproof cushion with the wheel moved closer.

## Material and world rules

- Painted sheet metal, bare steel, copper, ABS, rubber, timber and glass are
  separate materials. Rust belongs on exposed ferrous damage, not every surface.
- Metric object-space UVs keep scratches the same size and attached to moving
  parts. Sponsor plates have their own planar UVs, padding and correct aspect.
  Paint does not borrow a circuit-board tile; numbers do not wrap across edges.
- Physical seams, rivets, patches and scraped leading edges support the wear.
  Decals stay readable at garage distance; silhouettes stay readable while racing.
- Bliss progresses through intake/sorting, broken electronics windrows, cable
  recovery and repaired meadow. Reactor Gardens has cultivation, irrigation,
  energy and pump-service areas. Afterglow has transit, roof plant, freight and
  communications districts. Aurora has survey, snow-control, communications and
  rescue equipment. Props have a job and placement, not just random density.
